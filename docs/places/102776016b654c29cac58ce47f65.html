Maly Trostinec

After the first phase of deportations from the “Reich” and the “Protectorate” to Minsk had been completed in November 1941, a total of 16 trains carrying more than 15,000 people from Vienna, Königsberg, Theresienstadt, and Cologne arrived in Minsk between May and October 1942. In accordance with an order issued by the Chief of the Security Police and the SD, Reinhard Heydrich, the deportation victims were murdered immediately upon arrival. A pine woodland several kilometres from the estate of Maly Trostinec, a former collective farm (kolkhoz), was chosen as the execution site.

The executions followed a fixed pattern, usually involving 80 to 100 men, including members of the Schutzpolizei and Waffen-SS. After the trains arrived at the freight station in Minsk, usually between 4:00 and 7:00 in the morning, a group from the office of the Commander of the Security Police and SD (KdS) supervised the unloading of the newly arrived people and their luggage. The arrivals were then driven to a nearby assembly point, where another KdS group confiscated all money and valuables from the Jews. At this assembly point, officials also selected the few individuals — between 20 and 50 per transport — who appeared suitable for forced labour at the Maly Trostinec estate.

From a loading area at the edge of the assembly point, the deportees were transported by lorry to pits about 18 kilometres away. This procedure remained unchanged for the first eight transports. From August 1942 onward, the trains were routed via a siding directly to the vicinity of the estate, where unloading and selection then took place.

Deportees from the first transports were shot at the pits. From around the beginning of June 1942, three “gas vans” were also used.

Of the approximately 8,700 Austrian Jews deported to Maly Trostinec — around 8,500 deported from Vienna and 136 from the Theresienstadt ghetto — only 17 known survivors remained.
